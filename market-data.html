WHAT IT IS

What the SOX is.

The PHLX Semiconductor Index (SOX) tracks 30 of the largest U.S.-listed semiconductor companies, weighted by market capitalization. It is the benchmark that chip ETFs, futures, and options are built on — and the cleanest single number for the health of the chip sector.

It began on December 1, 1993 at a base value of 200.00. It is calculated and published by Nasdaq, and it is rebalanced quarterly in March, June, September, and December, and reconstituted annually in September.

The Nasdaq factsheet as of September 30, 2026 shows how concentrated the SOX is. NVIDIA alone carries an 11.35% weight, Broadcom 8.67%, and Micron 8.13%, followed by Intel (4.91%), Advanced Micro Devices (4.75%), Marvell (4.57%), Applied Materials (4.08%), KLA (4.06%), TSMC (4.02%), and Analog Devices (4.01%). Advanced Micro Devices and Analog Devices enter the top ten in this factsheet, replacing ASML and Lam Research. That concentration is the trade: the SOX is effectively a bet on a small set of large, cyclical technology companies, and its swings — including the factsheet's reported 1-year return of 98.26% and calendar-2026 year-to-date return of 78.29% as of September 30, 2026 — reflect that leverage in both directions.
